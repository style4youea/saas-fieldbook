Reditus: a practical guide to the B2B SaaS affiliate network

Reditus brings B2B software companies and affiliates into one SaaS-focused network. This guide explains what that means in practice, where the model is useful, and what affiliates should evaluate before choosing a program.

Research-based guideNot a hands-on platform reviewBy SaaS Fieldbook

Commercial relationships, when applicable, do not determine our conclusions. See our affiliate disclosure.

Affiliate marketing for software can become surprisingly fragmented. A publisher may need separate accounts, dashboards, tracking links and payout processes for every software company it promotes. SaaS vendors face the opposite problem: having an affiliate program does not automatically mean relevant partners will discover it.

Reditus is built around that gap. It describes itself as an affiliate management platform and network focused specifically on B2B SaaS. Software companies can run affiliate and referral programs, while affiliates can discover programs through a shared marketplace and manage their partnerships from one account.

What is Reditus?

At its core, Reditus serves two groups. SaaS companies use the platform to create, manage, track and grow affiliate programs. Affiliates use the network to find B2B software programs and earn commissions when their referrals become paying customers.

The specialization matters. Reditus is not positioned as a general marketplace for fashion, consumer goods or physical products. Its public materials focus on B2B software and recurring SaaS subscriptions. For an affiliate already writing about business software, ecommerce operations, marketing technology or productivity tools, that narrower scope can make discovery more relevant than browsing a general affiliate network.

How the marketplace works for affiliates

An affiliate can create a Reditus account for free and browse programs in the marketplace. According to Reditus documentation, a publisher can review a program's commission structure and duration, open additional product information, request to join and accept the individual partner terms. Some programs may approve applicants automatically; others review applications before an affiliate can begin promoting them.

This is an important distinction: joining Reditus does not mean automatic acceptance into every software company's affiliate program. The individual SaaS company can still set its own terms and approval process.

Why recurring SaaS commissions are interesting

Many software products are sold as monthly or annual subscriptions. That allows some affiliate programs to pay a percentage of recurring customer revenue for a defined period rather than a single one-time fee. Reditus explicitly positions recurring commissions as one of the advantages of B2B SaaS affiliate marketing.

Recurring does not automatically mean profitable, however. A high commission percentage on a product that rarely converts can be worth less than a lower rate on a product that closely matches the audience. The duration of the commission, customer price, cookie or attribution rules, refund terms and customer retention can all affect the real value of a program.

What should an affiliate check before joining a program?

A commission percentage is only the first line of the evaluation. Before publishing a recommendation, we would check at least these questions:

  • Audience fit: Does the software solve a problem your readers genuinely have?
  • Commission duration: Is the reward one-time, recurring for a fixed number of months, or lifetime?
  • Approval and promotion rules: Are there restrictions on paid ads, email, coupon sites, brand bidding or other traffic sources?
  • Product evidence: Can you verify the claims you intend to make through documentation, a trial or actual use?
  • Pricing and conversion friction: Is there a free trial, free tier or demo, and how large is the jump to a paid plan?
  • Attribution: How is a referral credited, and how long does the relevant tracking window last?
  • Payout conditions: When does commission become payable and what happens after cancellations or refunds?

The practical rule is simple: choose the product first and the commission second. Affiliate content becomes more useful when the recommendation would still make sense if the commission disappeared tomorrow.

What Reditus offers SaaS companies

For vendors, Reditus combines affiliate-program management with partner discovery. Its public product information describes tracking, affiliate recruitment, marketplace exposure, affiliate tiers, integrations and payout management. Reditus also promotes a network of more than 27,000 affiliates and says the network has generated millions of dollars in customer revenue.

Those network-level figures are vendor-reported and should be understood as platform-wide numbers, not a prediction of what an individual affiliate or SaaS company will earn.

Where Reditus appears strongest

The clearest advantage is focus. A software publisher can browse programs without sorting through unrelated consumer categories, and a B2B SaaS company can seek partners already interested in software. The marketplace can also reduce the administrative friction of maintaining unrelated affiliate accounts across many vendors.

That does not remove the hard part of affiliate marketing. An affiliate still needs useful content, qualified traffic and trust. A marketplace provides opportunities and tracking infrastructure; it does not manufacture buyer intent.

Potential limitations to keep in mind

Program quality will naturally vary because the marketplace contains offers from different companies with different products, prices and partner terms. A strong network therefore does not make every listed program a strong recommendation.

Affiliates should also avoid treating advertised commission percentages as guaranteed income. Earnings depend on traffic quality, conversion, attribution, customer retention and the individual program's rules. Product details and partner terms can change, so time-sensitive claims should be verified before publication.

Who is Reditus likely to suit?

Based on its structure, Reditus is most relevant to publishers, consultants, agencies, creators and other partners whose audiences buy business software. It may be particularly useful for content sites that publish SaaS reviews, comparisons, implementation guides or workflow-focused articles and want to research multiple programs from one place.

It is less compelling simply because someone wants “an affiliate program.” Without an audience or a credible distribution method, access to more offers does not solve the underlying acquisition problem.

SaaS Fieldbook verdict

Research verdict: Reditus has a clear proposition: concentrate B2B SaaS affiliate programs and partner management in a network designed around recurring software revenue. The marketplace is worth investigating for publishers already covering business software. The right way to use it is selectively—evaluate each product and its terms on their own merits rather than choosing offers by commission rate alone.

Sources and verification

This guide was researched from Reditus's public website and Help Center, including its platform overview, affiliate information, network statistics and documentation explaining how affiliates join SaaS programs. Facts were checked in September 2026. Because marketplace listings, program terms and platform features can change, readers should confirm current details directly with Reditus and the relevant SaaS vendor before making a commercial decision.
